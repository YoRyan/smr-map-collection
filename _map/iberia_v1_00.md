description: The first railway line in Spain dates from 1848, Barcelona-Mataro, about
  29 km, opened on October the 28th of that year. In 1849 Queen Isabel II inaugurated
  the Madrid-Aranjuez line, the section that began the first 45 km of what became
  the Madrid-Alicante line.  The railroad continued to spread out in Spain so that
  in less than two decades they were granted the majority of the fundamental lines
  of the Spanish system.  Between 1855 and 1868, the railroad system passed 305 km
  to a total of nearly 5,000 km.  The civil war came to an end in 1939 and many railroads
  and stations were destroyed.  The State nationalized the system by the passing of
  a law in 1941. This law it created, the National Network of Spanish Railroads (Red
  Nacional de los Ferrocarriles Espaoles; RENFE) which included all the railroads
  of the State, except those of the narrows lines.  Spain adopted the high speed railroad
  (BIRD) from the French high tech TGV (chosen over the German, Italian and Japanese),
  to widen its' international Madrid-Seville line in 1992, which achieved a speed
  of 300 km/h. Soon there was a second line between Madrid and Barcelona that reached
  the French border.
